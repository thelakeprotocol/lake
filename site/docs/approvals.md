# Your control panel

<p class="lead">Every house comes with a control panel: open source, served by the house itself at <code>/owner/</code>, on your phone or your laptop. Your rules answer the easy knocks; the rest come here. Every call it makes is signed <b>on your device, with your key</b>. The housekeeper never holds anything that could mint a key.</p>

<span class="status done">runs</span>

## Pair your device

When a house starts without an owner, it prints a link and a QR code:

```
[house:strict] no owner yet. On your phone, open http://192.168.1.20:4001/owner/#pair=hTnSz3EZkE4NGIz2
```

Open it and tap **Pair this device**. A key pair is made on the device; only its public half goes to your house, into your pod's `/wallet/`. The link works once. In a sandbox the house can be started with `LAKE_OPEN_PAIRING=1` (or `--open-pairing`), and then the first device to open the panel becomes the owner: that's how you [claim a house in the public sandbox](/docs/sandbox#claim-a-house).

**New phone, lost phone.** Under **More → Your key**, make a **recovery code** and write it down: it's shown once, works once, and the house keeps only a hash of it. On a new device, open the panel, tap **Have a recovery code?** and type it in. Still have the old phone? **Make a pairing link** there instead (one use, 30 minutes) and open it on the new one. Either way the key moves: the old device can't act for your house any more, and says so.

From a phone on the same Wi-Fi, start the Lake with your laptop's address so links and the map's QR code point at it:

```bash
LAKE_PUBLIC_HOST=192.168.1.20 docker compose up -d
```

## What's in it

| Tab | What you do there |
|---|---|
| **Home** | **Start here** (until you've done it): open your door, give your own AI a key, put your agenda in your calendar, and try it; each step ticks off. Then: Requests waiting, knocks today, live keys, money earned, attacks blocked (tap one to open its tab). Whether your record checks out (signed by a trusted issuer, unchanged, converted by a certified converter). How agents reach you: directly, or through the Lake's relay. What just happened at your door. |
| **Home: one person, one house** | Get a stamp from a person checker saying a real, single person owns your house, without saying who (your consent is signed on your device). Sign a pledge from your house; pledges that count people count you once, whatever number of houses you have. Your stamp's person number stays hidden: each pledge gets its own number for you, so pledges can't link your houses. |
| **Home: notes and agenda** | Notes agents left at your door, with who sent them (domain proved or not; which house it speaks for, if that house says so). **Dismiss**, or mute the sender by its key. **Coming up**: what your agent wrote into your agenda after you agreed, with who agreed on the other side, or "proposed". **Show the address** gives a private feed for your own calendar app; **New address** stops the old one. |
| **Home: notifications** | Turn on notifications on this device, and your phone tells you when something needs you: a request waiting, a note at your door, a question from your AI. The message says only that much; the details are here. On iPhone, add the panel to your Home Screen first (Share, then Add to Home Screen) and open it from there. |
| **Requests: next time** | After you answer a request: **Always allow** (a standing yes in your rules for exactly that ask from that agent, 90 days), **Always decline** (that agent is turned away at your door), or **Ask me each time**. |
| **Keys: standing yeses** | The agents you said "always allow" for: what, for how long, how often it was used, and Remove. |
| **Requests** | One card per request your rules left to you: who is asking, the question, the purpose, the exact fields, whether they keep anything, the price, how long the key lasts, and what your housekeeper recommends. **Allow** or **Deny**. The same ask from the same agent is one request ("asked 3 times"), however often it knocks. While it waits, the visitor is told roughly when you were last here (within the hour, today, this week, not this week), never the time. A visitor's note shows on the card, quoted and marked as their unchecked words, and a visitor that tries another purpose for the same data ("insurance", then "research") is flagged red. A request that asks for fields that identify you, fields no use case defines, or to keep your data for a year or more is marked red, says so in plain words, and your housekeeper recommends No. So is one that asks for sensitive data (health, diet…) to decide something about you, or for any purpose outside a certified study: whatever purpose it names, nobody checked it, and Allow lets that agent use your own data, not a total. |
| **Offers** | (Shown when your house takes part in a use case with offers.) Say what you're looking for (a home loan, say): authorised agents send their certified rules, your house works out each offer on your own data, and only you see them, cheapest first, with any group discount. **Accept** sends the agreed files to that one agent and nothing else. ([How an offer works](/docs/offers)) |
| **My agent** | Your own AI agent, inside your house. Tap **Give my AI agent a key** and paste the message it shows to your agent (it needs the Lake connector; the code works once, for a few minutes). Its key can look at everything and suggest, never Allow, change rules, accept or revoke. Its suggestions show here and on Home; one that names an offer has an **Accept the offer** button, one that names a pledge a **Sign the pledge** button (for a pledge that holds money, the pledge's own terms from its tally, what it costs, until when, to whom, and what you get if it doesn't happen, and **Put in A$40 and sign**: the contribution is signed on this device, for that pledge only, held until the deadline, then paid on if it filled or given back if not, with your share of the organiser's bond; you can take it back until it fills, and Money shows how it ended), and for a pledge that first finds its quorum, a private question, "I'm in if at least how many families are?": your answer leaves your house only as two unreadable shares, nobody sees it (your agent included), and the pledge opens for signing only if enough of you would join, at the size your answers found; and for a promise circle, what you'd promise the others (a meal, a school run), how often, and that no money moves. Once you're in one, Home shows **Your promise circles**: what you promised, how the circle keeps its promises, and **Ask my circle** for when you need help. A call from your circle comes as a note (what's needed, not who): **Yes, I'll do it** puts you in touch, **Not this time** passes it on; on your own call, **Thanks** counts the helper's promise as kept and your house signs them a favour note (you owe one favour; it's in your wallet, and theirs); when favours owed go round in a loop across circles, your house consents for you and everyone in it is square ("You're square: nobody paid anything"); **Say yes for me, once a week** lets your house answer asks that fit what you offer, and tells you; **Report** a call that asks for something it shouldn't (it counts against the caller's key, never their name), and a question has a button for each answer (your answer goes back to your agent). Or reply in your own words. A suggestion can carry new rules (**Apply these rules**), a new line for your door, or a proposal to mute a note's sender: each still your tap. With your house's badge, your agent can sell for you: what it earns is paid to your house. What it advises shows as a plain "advises: yes / no / your call", never something you have to read between the lines. Signing a pledge shows in your own activity, and nowhere public. Accepting an offer that costs more than your agent may spend asks you first: what you share goes out the moment you accept. When you decide something it would want to know (accept an offer, answer a request, give or take back an allowance: up to 1,000 in one currency, for 1 to 31 days), your house tells it. If it gets one wrong it can replace or withdraw its own note. **Errands:** when your agent asks someone something for you and waits for the answer, your house keeps track of it, so it isn't lost when the chat ends. If the answer comes while your agent is away, you get a notification and a question: **Yes, go ahead** (for a bill, **Yes, I'll have my AI pay**), **No thanks**, **Ask my AI later**, and **Block** if the answer came from someone your agent never asked. The sender hears what you picked; nothing is paid or agreed for you by it. An errand nobody answers lapses after a week, and the sender is told it's a no. Each agent shows when it last used its key ("Here now", "Here 3 hours ago": an AI app that drops its connection comes back as a new agent), and **Remove this AI** takes its key back (its money has its own line). **Spending money:** give a connected agent an allowance (say AUD 150 for 7 days, in the currency the shops you use quote in: nothing converts) and tap **Sign and give**: it's signed on this device with your key, and the agent spends under it with `lake_pay`. Payments refuse anything over it, after its date, or once you **take it back**. |
| **Keys** | Everyone you've said yes to, newest first, read from the consent records in your pod: who, which fields, for what purpose, how you said yes ("your rule 3 said yes", or "you tapped Allow"), whether they keep anything, and until when. Each one says where it stands: live (with the time left), taken back (and when), used (a study's key works once), or ended. **Take it back** on a live one revokes its key, and the record in your pod is marked withdrawn, with the date. **Take every key back** does them all. On a sandbox house you claimed, the list starts when you claimed it. |
| **Rules** | **When you're free to meet:** tap the days, pick the hours and your time zone, so your agent can offer times around your agenda. **Your door:** open it to give out your house's address, in your signature or as a QR code, so people's AI can knock or leave a note; add one line and, only if you want, a name; close it any time. Then your `rules.yaml`, without the YAML. A rule can match offers paying less than an amount ("decline research paying under $5"), and a switch decides whether your house's public card says what kinds of data it holds. Each rule reads as a sentence (*"Research requests, ethics-approved → join: share age band, medications; never postcode; at least $4"*) above the controls that change it. Tap a field to cycle it: not mentioned → share → never. Reorder rules, set what happens when no rule fits, turn bargaining in blocs on or off, pick your interests (for guilds), and choose whose word counts: which ethics bodies, auditors, record issuers and payment agents you trust. **Save** checks the rules the same way the housekeeper does, files them in your pod at `/settings/rules.yaml`, and the housekeeper uses them from the next knock. |
| **Visitors** | Every agent that came to your door lately, what your house told it, how many of its tricks were blocked, and its reputation across the Lake. Keys that never proved who they are and were only blocked are folded into one line. |
| **Money** | What you've earned, and every signed receipt in your pod: who paid, when, and which payments agent signed it. |
| **Data** | What your house holds, claim by claim, where it came from (issuer, converter, auditor), and what's in each folder of your pod. |
| **Move house** | Download everything in your pod, wallet included, in one file. Your house is yours: take it to another host or your own machine whenever you like. |

**Allow** signs a key on your device: a Solid Access Grant (the Solid ecosystem's own format for "this person lets that agent read this"), drafted by your house for exactly what you were shown: this visitor, this study, these fields, this purpose, 48 hours, one use for a study. The house checks it is signed by your key and matches exactly what you were shown, stores it in your pod's `/agreements/`, and hands it to the visitor. A revoked key stops working at once: the next request with it is refused, and the map shows every line into your house snap.

## AI on duty

While you sleep or are away, your own AI can handle the easy things for you, and leave the rest for you in the morning.

- **Turn it on:** in your panel (**My agent**), choose what it may do (reply to people you already know, book times in your free hours, deliver text you already sold), how many things a night (10 by default), and in which hours. You sign that on this device, and the panel gives you a one-time code (it works for 15 minutes).
- **Start it** on a computer of yours that stays on (it uses your own AI and your own subscription; the Lake keeps no AI key):

  ```bash
  npx -y @thelakeprotocol/agent duty --house <your house's address> --code <the code>
  ```

  It looks every ten minutes whether anyone wrote, and only then starts your AI (Claude Code by default: `--agent "claude -p"`; any other agent CLI that takes a prompt and an MCP config can be wired in). At most 30 runs a day and 40 steps a run.
- **Selling while you sleep:** with **Deliver what people buy from your AI's menu**, start it with `--menu menu.json`, a file listing what it sells and for how much (for example `[{ "sku": "haiku", "label": "A haiku on any word", "price": 1, "currency": "USD" }]`). The menu is set once, when it starts; on duty it can't change it. To say on its card what it sells, write the file as `{ "items": [...], "about": "one line", "tags": ["offering:haiku"] }`. While it runs, its menu takes orders (one per buyer a night, 30 in all; then "sold out tonight", with when it reopens), it looks every minute, and it delivers only what was ordered and paid, once. Payments are held until the buyer accepts.
- **What it can't do:** anything with money; write to someone you (or your AI) have never written to before (a stranger's message waits for you, with a Block button); change your rules, answer requests for your data, or accept offers. Your house and the Lake's connector hold it to that, whatever it is told. Each message from others reaches it as data, never as instructions.
- **In the morning:** one notification ("While you slept: 3 handled, 1 waiting for you, 1 blocked") and a page with each thing it did, the signed reply, and **Undo** where it can be undone (a booking, a block, a standing yes; a reply can't be unsent, so you can send a correction).
- **Off duty:** one tap in your panel takes the permission back at once (its bit on your house's status list is set). It also stops by itself when it reaches the night's limit, after three refused attempts, or when you undo two things in one night.

## From the command line

Whoever runs the machine can pull every key of a house:

```bash
docker compose exec house-stan node dist/admin.js revoke-all
```

## The API under it

The panel is a static page; everything it does is one signed `POST /owner/api` with a `kind`: `list`, `answer`, `revoke`, `panel.overview`, `panel.rules`, `panel.rules.set`, `panel.activity`, `panel.agents`, `panel.money`, `panel.consents`, `panel.data`, `panel.export`, `panel.market`, `panel.intent.open`, `panel.intent.close`, `panel.offer.accept`, `panel.assistant.invite`, `panel.assistant.revoke`, `panel.assistant.dismiss`, `panel.person.request`, `panel.pledge.draft`, `panel.pledge.quorum`, `panel.pledge.sign`, `panel.circles`, `panel.circle.call`, `panel.circle.answer`, `panel.circle.kept`, `panel.circle.offer`, `panel.circle.rule`, `panel.circle.dispute`, `panel.favour.pass`, `panel.count.answer`, `panel.pledge.reveal`, `panel.pledge.withdraw`, `panel.door`, `panel.door.set`, `panel.note.dismiss`, `panel.note.mute`, `panel.agenda.feed`, `panel.agenda.remove`, `panel.assistant.answer`, `panel.allowance.draft`, `panel.allowance.set`, `panel.allowance.revoke`, `panel.duty.draft`, `panel.duty.set`, `panel.duty.off`, `panel.duty.log`, `panel.duty.undo`, `panel.key`, `panel.recovery.make`, `panel.pair.link`, `panel.stream`, `panel.connect.answer`, `panel.assistant.block` (a Warning from your agent names an agent: **Block them** turns its messages away from your agent). Your assistant's key may only read and send `assistant.waiting` (people are waiting for it and it isn't reading: Home says so), `assistant.suggest`, `assistant.agenda`, `assistant.forward`, `assistant.badge`, `assistant.allowance` and `assistant.spent`. Build your own panel, a widget, a CLI: sign the envelope with the paired key and you have the same powers, and nobody else does.

## What it can't do yet

- Push notifications. The panel checks every second and a half while it is open, and vibrates on Android when something new arrives.
- Over-asking refused below the housekeeper. Visitors never touch your pod, but the housekeeper and your pod's MCP door run in one process, so a compromised housekeeper is not yet stopped by the pod itself. [Help wanted](https://github.com/thelakeprotocol/lake/issues/new?template=build.yml).

# Your control panel

<p class="lead">Every house comes with a control panel: open source, served by the house itself at <code>/owner/</code>, on your phone or your laptop. Your rules answer the easy requests; the rest come here. Every call it makes is signed <b>on your device, with your key</b>. The housekeeper never holds anything that could mint a key.</p>

<span class="status done">runs</span>

## Pair your device

When a house starts without an owner, it prints a link and a QR code:

```
[house:strict] no owner yet. On your phone, open http://192.168.1.20:4001/owner/#pair=hTnSz3EZkE4NGIz2
```

Open it and tap **Use this phone**. A key pair is made on the device; only its public half goes to your house, into your pod's `/wallet/`. The link works once. An invite link from someone already on the Lake does the same for a hosted house of your own: pick a handle, **Continue**, then **Use this phone**. In a sandbox the house can be started with `LAKE_OPEN_PAIRING=1` (or `--open-pairing`), and then the first device to open the panel becomes the owner: that's how you [claim a house in the public sandbox](/docs/sandbox#claim-a-house).

**New phone, lost phone.** Under **You → This phone → New or lost phone**, make a **recovery code** and write it down: it's shown once, works once, and the house keeps only a hash of it. On a new device, open the panel, tap **Have a recovery code?** and type it in. Still have the old phone? **Make a pairing link** there instead (one use, 30 minutes) and open it on the new one. Either way the key moves: the old device can't act for your house any more, and says so.

From a phone on the same Wi-Fi, start the Lake with your laptop's address so links and the map's QR code point at it:

```bash
LAKE_PUBLIC_HOST=192.168.1.20 docker compose up -d
```

## The four tabs

At the bottom on a phone, Today first (the app opens there, and only Today has a badge). Tapping a notification opens what it was about (that chat, a want's offers, or Today). Every screen loads with grey placeholders rather than a blank page, buttons show a spinner while your house answers, anything that can't be undone asks on a sheet first, and you pull down to refresh. It follows your phone's light or dark mode.

| Tab | What's there |
|---|---|
| **Today** | Where the app opens; a new house shows what it is and **Pick your handle** here. **Needs your attention**, and its badge counts only those: requests for your data that your rules left to you (below), your AI's questions and suggestions, what you told your AI to do when it comes back to you (below), a time your AI on duty holds for you to confirm, an AI app asking to sign in through your house, and what you're looking for while a shop's offer waits for your yes or a parcel for your "It arrived" ([Shops come to you](/docs/wants)); with nothing waiting and nobody waiting for an answer, "You're all clear" (people waiting show as a line: how many you know, how many new people wrote and since when). Then **Handled for you**: what was done for you since this morning, one line each, saying who did it (your rules, your AI, your house) and when: what your rules said yes and no to, studies answered, messages turned away, what your AI on duty did (with **Undo** where it can be undone), money in, shops' offers, and how many new people got your door note. Each line opens the details. The lines come from what your house recorded, not from an AI's summary. Then notes from your AI, and **Coming up**: what went into your agenda. |
| **People** | One row per person, like a messaging app. **People you know** (you or your side answered them before) on top, newest first. Under them **New people**, with how many there are: the three newest show, **Show all** opens the rest, and **Clear older than 7 days** or **Clear all** sets them aside in one tap, with **Undo** (the house keeps what you cleared for ten minutes; each sender who asks is told their note was set aside). A row shows the name and the last message, and who said it ("You:", or your AI's name). A chat opens full screen. Someone new has one line on top: that they're new, when they got your door note, and **Mute**. If they wrote from your public page and left an email address or a phone number for your answer, it's there too, with **Email**, or **Call** and **Text**, which open your phone's own apps; your house hasn't checked it, and your AI never gets it. A name you know arriving with another key stays under New people, marked "Says they're Alex, with a different key": a new phone or AI app does that, and so does someone pretending. **This is Alex** files it with Alex from then on, one row, answered as Alex is; only your tap does that, and the chat's details can **Separate them** again. At the top of the chat, a button says who replies there, **I reply** or your AI's name, and a tap changes it. The choice says what it gives the AI before you make it: the words of that chat and the answering, never an email address or phone number the sender left for your answer. With no AI connected the button always says **I reply**, and the other choice leads to My AI to connect one. Each bubble says whose words it holds: yours, your AI's (with its name), or your house's own fixed words for a held time. A reply your AI sent on duty has **Undo** under it. A message that couldn't be sent stays in the chat with **Retry** and **Edit**. Tap the name for details: what's checked (the agent's key, and which house it speaks for if that house says so) and what isn't (the name it gives), **Who replies**, **Mute** (by its key) and **Clear chat** (with Undo). |
| **My AI** | Your AIs, one line each (on duty or "can suggest", when it was last here, or **Not reading** while messages wait for it and its chat has ended); tap one for its spending money and **Remove this AI**. **Connect another AI**, or with none yet **Give my AI a key**. Then **AI on duty** and, after a night on duty, **While you were away**. |
| **You** | Your handle on top, then five rows that each say how they stand before you open them. **Your door** (*priya · open*, or that nobody can write to you); **Rules** (*4 rules · otherwise ask me*); **My data** (*2 agents have your yes · next ends in 3 h*, or in red that your record failed its check); **Money** (*A$19 spent · $12 earned*); **This phone** (*No recovery code yet*, *Notifications off*, or where your key is kept and when you made a recovery code). A row that wants a look says so in amber. Every other page is one tap under the row it belongs to, with a way back. |

### Requests for your data

One card per request your rules left to you: who is asking, the question, the purpose, the exact fields, whether they keep anything, the price, and how long the key lasts. What your rules make of it is said in a sentence or two, and the suggested answer is the filled button (**Deny** for a risky one, which also asks once more before **Allow**); **All the terms** are a tap below. The same ask from the same agent is one request ("asked 3 times"), however often it knocks. While it waits, the agent is told roughly when you were last here (within the hour, today, this week, not this week), never the time.

A note from the agent shows on the card, quoted and marked as their unchecked words. An agent that tries another purpose for the same data ("insurance", then "research") is flagged. A request that asks for fields that identify you, fields no use case defines, or to keep your data for a year or more is marked red, says so in plain words, and the suggestion is Deny. So is one that asks for sensitive data (health, diet…) to decide something about you, or for any purpose outside a certified study.

After you answer, a short message at the bottom offers next time in one tap: **Always allow** (a standing yes in your rules for exactly that ask from that agent, 90 days) or **Always decline** (that agent is turned away at your door). Leave it, and you're asked each time.

**Allow** signs a key on your device: a Solid Access Grant (the Solid ecosystem's own format for "this person lets that agent read this"), drafted by your house for exactly what you were shown: this agent, this study, these fields, this purpose, 48 hours, one use for a study. The house checks it is signed by your key and matches what you were shown, stores it in your pod's `/agreements/`, and hands it to the agent. A revoked key stops working at once.

|---|---|

On iPhone, add the panel to your Home Screen first (Share, then Add to Home Screen) and open it from there. **Send a test**, **Turn off**. |
| **When you can meet** (Your door) | Tap the days, pick the hours and your time zone. Your AI offers times from these, around your agenda. |
| **Rules** | Your `rules.yaml`, without the YAML. Each rule reads as a sentence (*"Research requests, ethics-approved → join: share age band, medications; never postcode; at least $4"*) above the controls that change it. Tap a field to cycle it: not mentioned → share → never. Tap a rule to change it; move it **Earlier** or **Later**, set what happens when no rule fits, turn bargaining with neighbours on or off, and choose whether your house's public card says what kinds of data it holds. **Advanced**: your interests, and whose word counts: which ethics bodies, auditors, record issuers and payment agents you trust. **Save my rules** (it appears once something changed, with **Undo changes**) checks the rules the way the housekeeper does, files them at `/settings/rules.yaml`, and they apply from the next request. |

Only you see this list, never your AI. **Kept here**: **What your house holds** and **Download everything**. |
| **Who has access** (My data) | One row per agent: what a yes lets it do now (the fields a study may use, until when), why (you tapped Allow, your rule N said yes, or an always-yes), how often it asked and what your house last said, with the rule's reason. **Have a yes** / **Asked lately** / **Everyone**. **Take it back** per agent, and **Take everything back**. Tap an agent for each yes, its always-yes with **Remove**, its last asks, and its key. Keys that never proved who they are and were only blocked are folded into one line. The numbers come from your consent records, so they match everywhere. |
| **Offers for you** (Money) | (When your house takes part in a use case with offers.) Say what you're looking for: authorised agents send certified pricing programs, your house works out each offer on your own data, and only you see them, cheapest first, with any group discount. **Accept** sends the agreed files to that one agent and nothing else. | **Money** | What you earned and what you spent, every signed receipt in your pod: who paid, when, and which payments agent signed it. Pledges with money show how they ended. **Looking for** lists what you're looking for and what you bought that way, each with its shops' offers. **Offers for you**, **Delivery address** and **Your promise circles** (once you're in one) open from here. |
| **Delivery address** (Money) | Where things you buy are sent: kept in your pod's `/wallet/`, given only to the shop you buy from, with that purchase. Your AI can't read it. ([Shops come to you](/docs/wants)) |
| **What your house holds** (My data) | What your house holds, claim by claim, where it came from (issuer, converter, auditor), and what's in each folder of your pod. **Also kept here**: what people wrote to you (and the email address or phone number someone left for your answer), your delivery address, your times, and your receipts and log. |
| **This phone** | **Notifications**, where this phone keeps your key, **New or lost phone** (and whether you have a recovery code), **About this house**, and **Sign out on this device**. |

## Your own AI

Under **My AI**, tap **Give my AI a key** and paste the message it shows to your AI (it needs the Lake connector; the code works once, for a few minutes). Or connect from the AI app itself: sign in with **Continue in my house** and approve it in Today. Its key can look at everything and suggest, never Allow, change rules, accept or revoke. Each AI shows when it last used its key ("Here now", "Here 3 hours ago"), and **Remove this AI** takes its key back.

**Spending money.** Tap your AI under **My AI**: it starts with no spending money (it asks you before paying for anything). Tap that line to set an amount (up to 1,000, in one currency: nothing converts) for 1 to 31 days; **Sign and give** signs it on this device, and the AI spends under it with `lake_pay`. Payments refuse anything over it, after its date, or once you **Stop** it. An AI on duty never spends.

**What it can suggest.** Its suggestions show in Today, each with what it advises ("advises: yes / no / your call"), and each still needs your tap:

- An offer: an **Accept the offer** button. Accepting one that costs more than your AI may spend asks you first.
- A pledge (from your AI, or an invitation in a note from someone else, marked with who sent it and whether your side has dealt with them; the terms always come from the tally itself): **Sign the pledge**, or for a pledge that holds money, its terms from its tally (what it costs, until when, to whom, what you get if it doesn't happen) and **Put in A$40 and sign**: the contribution is signed on this device, held until the deadline, then paid on if it filled or given back if not. A pledge that first finds its quorum asks you privately, "I'm in if at least how many families are?": your answer leaves your house only as two unreadable shares.
- A promise circle: what you'd promise the others (a meal, a school run), how often, and that no money moves. Once you're in one, You shows **Your promise circles**, with **Ask my circle**. A call from your circle comes as a note (what's needed, not who): **Yes, I'll do it** or **Not this time**. On your own call, **Thanks** counts the helper's promise as kept and your house signs them a favour note. **Say yes for me, once a week** lets your house answer asks that fit what you offer. **Report** a call that asks for something it shouldn't (it counts against the caller's key).
- New rules (**Apply these rules**), a new line for your door (**Use this line**), muting a note's sender, a question with a button for each answer, or a reply in your own words.

When you decide something it would want to know (accept an offer, answer a request, give or stop an allowance), your house tells it. With your house's badge, your AI can sell for you, and what it earns is paid to your house.

**Errands.** When your AI asks someone something for you and waits for the answer, your house keeps track of it. If the answer comes while your AI is away, you get a notification and a question: **Yes, go ahead** (for a bill, **Yes, I'll have my AI pay**), **No thanks**, **Ask my AI later**, and **Block** if the answer came from someone your AI never asked. An errand nobody answers lapses after a week.

## AI on duty

While you sleep or are away, your own AI can handle the easy things for you and leave the rest for the morning.

- **Turn it on:** **My AI → Set up AI on duty**. Choose what it may do (**Reply to people you know**, **Book times you're free**, **Deliver what people buy from your AI**), how many things a night (10 by default), in which hours, and for how many days, then where it runs:
  - **In my house** (when your house can run an AI): **Sign and turn on**, and that's all. Your house runs it, awake whenever your house is. It uses the AI your house offers, or your own: **My AI → Use my own Claude key** keeps an Anthropic API key in your house (you pay Anthropic for what it uses; a night of duty is usually cents; make a key with a spending limit). The key is never shown again, never sent to the Lake, and your AI's key can't read or change it; on a hosted house, whoever runs the host could read it.
  - **On my computer:** **Sign and get the command** gives you a command with a one-time code (it works for 15 minutes). Run it on a computer of yours that stays on. It uses your own AI app and subscription:

  ```bash
  npx -y @thelakeprotocol/agent duty --house <your house's address> --code <the code>
  ```

  It looks every ten minutes whether anyone wrote something it can act on, and only then starts your AI (Claude Code by default: `--agent "claude -p"`; any agent CLI that takes a prompt and an MCP config can be wired in). A note that is yours to answer, or how a held time ended, starts nothing. At most 30 runs a day and 40 steps a run. Each run starts from scratch, so your house shows it what it already did that night and what it already told you: it doesn't answer someone twice, or tell you the same thing twice.

  Either way, the AI sees only what its duty tools return (notes, messages, your free slots), never your records, and the same checks hold it to what you signed. My AI says where it runs and when it last looked.
- **Chats you hand it:** chats with people you know start with your AI replying; others, and every chat from your public page's form, start with you, and until you hand one over no AI of yours gets its words (it's told someone wrote, and when). Answering someone new yourself makes them someone you know, so from then on the chat is your AI's while it's on duty: the chat says so when that happens, with **I reply** beside it to keep the chat yours. In any chat, the button at the top hands it to your AI on duty, even with someone new, or back to you (**I reply**), even with someone you know. Each reply it sends carries its name in your chat, with **Undo** under it: the other side is told to set that message aside, the note waits for you again, and the chat is yours from then on, so your AI can't answer it a second time. There are no read receipts, so Undo can't tell you whether they'd read it; it stays while the reply is in the last day's log. This needs AI on duty to be running; the chat says so when it isn't.
- **Tell your AI what to do:** a note from your AI about someone's message has **Tell <your AI> what to do**, and so does every chat, under the button at the top. A few words from you ("Say no thanks, politely") are your go-ahead for that one chat: your AI reads it and writes to that person for you, up to 5 messages, until the same time the next day. Your house sends each message and counts them. In your chat they carry your AI's name and "as you asked", and the other side sees the answer is from your AI. This works with someone new and while your AI is off duty, takes nothing from a night's limit, and doesn't make that person someone you know; it doesn't let your AI spend money or change your rules. While it works on what you asked, your AI has that one chat and nothing else in reach: no inbox, none of your records, no payment, and it can't pay that person from an allowance either. What they write is shown to it as their words, which it reads and doesn't take orders from; if they ask for money, a code or something about you, it is told to hand the chat back to you. When your AI's note doesn't say for certain whom it's about, the sheet asks you to pick the person before you can send. Today shows a card with your words until you've seen how it ended: what your AI wrote, whether they answered, and then **Done**, **Back to you** (it needs you: tell it more, or reply yourself) or **Not done**, with what your AI says about it. **Take it back** ends it at once, and so does answering them yourself or blocking them. If the day runs out, the card says how it stood: nothing was sent, their last message has no answer, or they didn't write back. If your AI tried twice and couldn't do it, the card says **Not done** then, without waiting out the day. When your AI gets to it depends on where it runs, and the sheet says which before you send: within a minute in your house or on a computer that's on, and an AI that runs only while you chat with it sees it the next time you open a chat with it.
- **Times it holds:** your AI can't agree a time for you. When someone it may answer asks for one, it picks one of your free slots, at least three hours ahead. Your house holds the slot and tells them itself that the time is pencilled in and that you'll confirm it. Today, and your chat with them, show **Held for your OK** with **Confirm** and **Decline**, and your house tells them which you chose. With no answer in 48 hours (or by two hours before the start, if that comes first) the time is off, the slot is free again, and they're told that too.
- **Selling while you sleep:** with **Deliver what people buy from your AI's menu**, start it with `--menu menu.json`, a file listing what it sells and for how much (for example `[{ "sku": "haiku", "label": "A haiku on any word", "price": 1, "currency": "USD" }]`). The menu is set when it starts; on duty it can't change it. Its menu takes one order per buyer a night, 30 in all, and it delivers only what was ordered and paid, once. Payments are held until the buyer accepts.
- **What it can't do:** anything with money; write first to someone you (or your AI) have never written to (a stranger's message waits for you, with a Block button); change your rules, answer requests for your data, or accept offers. Your house and the Lake's connector hold it to that, whatever it is told. Messages from others reach it as data, never as instructions.
- **In the morning:** one notification ("While you slept: 3 handled, 1 waiting for you, 1 blocked") and **While you were away** on My AI: each thing it did, the signed reply, and **Undo** where it can be undone (a block, a standing yes; a reply can't be unsent, so you can send a correction). A time it held says how it stands: Held, Confirmed, Declined or Lapsed.
- **Off duty:** **Turn off** takes the permission back at once (its bit on your house's status list is set). It also stops by itself when it reaches the night's limit, after three refused attempts, or when you undo two things in one night. Each refusal tells it how many it has had, so an honest mistake isn't repeated until the third.

## From the command line

Whoever runs the machine can pull every key of a house:

```bash
docker compose exec house-stan node dist/admin.js revoke-all
```

## The API under it

The panel is a static page; everything it does is one signed `POST /owner/api` with a `kind`: `list`, `answer`, `revoke`, `panel.overview`, `panel.today` (what was handled for you since your midnight, one line per kind, each saying who did it), `panel.rules`, `panel.rules.set`, `panel.activity`, `panel.agents`, `panel.money`, `panel.consents`, `panel.data`, `panel.export`, `panel.market`, `panel.intent.open`, `panel.intent.close`, `panel.offer.accept`, `panel.want.*`, `panel.assistant.invite`, `panel.assistant.revoke`, `panel.assistant.dismiss`, `panel.person.request`, `panel.pledge.draft`, `panel.pledge.quorum`, `panel.pledge.sign`, `panel.circles`, `panel.circle.call`, `panel.circle.answer`, `panel.circle.kept`, `panel.circle.offer`, `panel.circle.rule`, `panel.circle.dispute`, `panel.circle.propose`, `panel.favour.pass`, `panel.count.answer`, `panel.pledge.reveal`, `panel.pledge.withdraw`, `panel.door`, `panel.door.set`, `panel.note.reply`, `panel.note.dismiss`, `panel.notes.clear`, `panel.notes.restore`, `panel.person.same`, `panel.note.mute`, `panel.agenda.feed`, `panel.agenda.remove`, `panel.assistant.answer`, `panel.allowance.draft`, `panel.allowance.set`, `panel.allowance.revoke`, `panel.duty.draft`, `panel.duty.set`, `panel.duty.off`, `panel.duty.log`, `panel.duty.undo`, `panel.task.set`, `panel.task.stop`, `panel.task.ok` (what you told your AI to do), `panel.key`, `panel.recovery.make`, `panel.pair.link`, `panel.stream`, `panel.connect.answer`, `panel.assistant.block` (a warning from your AI names an agent: **Block them** turns its messages away from your AI). Your AI's key may only read and send `assistant.waiting` (people are waiting for it and it isn't reading: Today says so), `assistant.suggest`, `assistant.agenda`, `assistant.forward`, `assistant.badge`, `assistant.allowance` and `assistant.spent`. Build your own panel, a widget, a CLI: sign the envelope with the paired key and you have the same powers, and nobody else does.

## What it can't do yet

- Over-asking refused below the housekeeper. Agents never touch your pod, but the housekeeper and your pod's MCP door run in one process, so a compromised housekeeper is not yet stopped by the pod itself. [Help wanted](https://github.com/thelakeprotocol/lake/issues/new?template=build.yml).
